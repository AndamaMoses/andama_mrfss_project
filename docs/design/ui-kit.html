01

Identity

A pharmacy runs on records. The interface should feel like those records are being handled with care. Warm paper, dark ink, brass rules, precise labels — confidence without decoration.

The visual metaphor is the ledger and the label. This is not a nostalgic paper pastiche; it is a precise operational instrument that borrows the confidence of the ledger: ruled alignment, tabular figures, explicit labels, and a clear record of who did what.

Principles

Six rules that settle arguments
01

The record comes first.

A screen exists to help someone find, change, or trust a record. Decoration that does not help that job is removed.

02

Label it like a pharmacist.

Say “Expires in 5 days”, not “Attention required”. Use plain, specific words and put the number where the eye lands first.

03

Structure with rules, not boxes.

Hairlines, alignment, and spacing create groups. A border is a last resort, not the default container.

04

Brass marks importance.

A seal, an active marker, a focus ring, one underline. If everything is brass, nothing is.

05

Density is a service.

Counter staff need many rows on screen. Keep 40–44 px controls and compact tables, then relax on mobile rather than shrinking everything.

06

No false affordances.

If it looks like a button it is a button. A status is a stamp, not a pill that invites a tap.

Typesetting rules

Six rules, no exceptions
  • Display type is structural, not decorative. One page title, one section opening. Never a whole paragraph in Fraunces.
  • Sentence case for nearly everything. Uppercase is reserved for mono labels and status stamps.
  • Numbers lead. “3 batches expire this week”, not “This week, three batches are expiring”.
  • Tabular numerals always. Use font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums for money, quantities, dates, counts.
  • No text shadows, no letter-spaced body copy, no centred paragraphs longer than two lines.
  • Line length 60–75 characters for prose, 45–60 for narrow panels. text-wrap: balance on headings only.

  2. State the consequence.80 units leave sale and become a write-off.

Motion & access

Motion confirms a change; it does not perform personality. Accessibility is the floor: visible focus, real labels, 40–44 px targets, and status that never relies on colour alone.

No bounce, spring, parallax, or looping decoration. The live dot pulses at 2 s and stops under prefers-reduced-motion.
Focus 2 px · 2 px offset
A link

Press Tab through this specimen. The ring is brass-600 at 2 px with a 2 px offset.

Keyboard order follows visual order. Dialogs trap focus and restore it on close. Never remove an outline without replacing it.

Access checklist

Before any screen ships
  • Contrast: 4.5:1 body, 3:1 large text and UI marks. The pairs in section 02 are the approved set.
  • Focus: every interactive element has a visible ring in visual order; dialogs trap and restore focus.
  • Targets: 40 × 40 px desktop minimum, 44 × 44 px on touch.
  • Labels: persistent visible labels; placeholders are examples, not labels.
  • Errors: announced (role="alert"), described, and tied to the field with aria-describedby.
  • Status: word + mark + colour, never colour alone.
  • Zoom: 200% must not clip content or hide actions; test at 320 px width.
  • Tables and charts: captions, scope, and a text summary for every chart.

Six steps · one visual language

Definition of done

For every screen
  • Semantic tokens only; no raw hex in component CSS.
  • One primary action; hierarchy obvious at arm’s length.
  • Numbers tabular and aligned; currency and dates follow the rules.
  • Empty, loading, error, and permission-denied states designed.
  • Keyboard focus visible and in visual order.
  • Works at 320 px and at 200% zoom.
  • No emoji, glass, gradient mesh, floating card, or pill button.
  • Copy reviewed against the voice rules.
